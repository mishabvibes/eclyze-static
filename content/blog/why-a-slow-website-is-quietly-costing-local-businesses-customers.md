## How a slow website drags customers away

A slow website feels like a slow train. Customers get impatient and often leave before they even see what you have to offer.

## The cost of a slow load time

[Akamai's State of Online Retail Performance report](https://www.akamai.com/newsroom/press-release/akamai-releases-spring-2017-state-of-online-retail-performance-report) found that a **100-millisecond** delay in page load time can cut conversion rates by as much as 7% — and the effect compounds as delays grow into whole seconds. For a local shop that earns ₹5,000 a day from online orders, even a couple of seconds of extra load time can plausibly cost several hundred rupees a day in lost orders — which adds up fast over a month.

## Real‑world impact for local businesses

Take Sree Bakery in Thrissur, for example. Their website used to load in 2.5 seconds, but after a recent update it took 5 seconds. Within a month, online orders fell by 30% and they lost 15 regular customers who switched to a competitor with a faster site.

A small boutique in Ernakulam faced a similar problem. Their page speed score dropped from 85 to 45, and foot traffic to the store dropped by 12% because potential buyers were frustrated by the sluggish experience.

## What causes slow load times

### Large images

High‑resolution images are great for marketing, but if they aren't compressed they can weigh a page down. A single 2‑MB JPEG can add a full second to the load time.

### Unoptimized code

Excessive CSS, JavaScript, and unused plugins bloat the codebase. Each line of code that the browser must parse adds latency.

### Poor hosting

Shared hosting plans often share bandwidth and CPU with dozens of other sites. If one of those sites experiences a traffic spike, your site can slow down.

## Quick wins to speed up

### Compress images

Use tools like TinyPNG or ImageOptim to reduce image size by up to 70% without noticeable quality loss.

### Enable caching

Set proper HTTP cache headers so repeat visitors load the site from local memory instead of re‑downloading every asset.

### Choose a reliable host

Opt for a host that offers dedicated resources or a managed WordPress solution. A good host can shave off half a second from the load time.

## Long‑term strategy

### Responsive design

A mobile‑friendly layout not only improves speed but also keeps users on the page. Google prioritises mobile‑first indexing, so a responsive site is a must.

### Regular audits

Run speed tests every six months with tools like GTmetrix or Lighthouse. Address the top three issues each time to keep the site snappy.
